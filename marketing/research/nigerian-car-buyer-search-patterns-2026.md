## 3. TOPIC-SPECIFIC RESEARCH

### Topic A: Tokunbo Car Inspection Tips

**Search Volume Indicators:** Multiple dedicated articles from Pulse Nigeria, 234Drive, Carlots.ng, Carbon365, Naijacarlovers, Autokunbo — indicating high demand.

**Key Content Points That Resonate:**
- VIN verification: Check the 17-digit code on dashboard, door jamb, and engine bay — must match customs papers
- Magnet test: Use a magnet on body panels to detect filler from accident repairs
- Underbody inspection: Rust, welding marks, or frame damage from accidents
- Cold-start engine test: Start engine cold, listen for knocking; blue smoke = burning oil, white smoke = coolant leak
- Interior age vs. odometer: New upholstery + worn steering/pedals = tampered odometer
- Test drive for 20-30 minutes minimum to reveal hidden issues
- "First body" verification: Check for overspray, mismatched bolts, panel gaps
- Flooded car detection: Radio/AC/reverse camera behaving erratically

### Topic B: Car Import Duty Nigeria 2026

**Search Volume Indicators:** Dedicated calculator tools exist (Carlots.ng, ImportDutyCalculator.com), government pages, and clearing agent sites — very high search demand.

**Key Data Points:**
- Import duty: 20% of CIF value
- NAC Levy: 15%
- Surcharge: 7% on duty amount
- VAT: 7.5% on gross total
- ETL: 0.5%
- Exchange rate used by customs: $1 = N1,450 (subject to change)
- New VIN valuation policy has increased costs for "underaged" vehicles (2016 and older)
- Total effective duty can reach 35%+ of CIF value

**Pain Points:**
- Constantly changing exchange rates make cost prediction difficult
- VIN valuation policy confusion
- Unclear total cost calculation — buyers don't know the final amount until they're committed
- Cotonou route vs. direct import cost comparison is confusing

**Content Opportunity for Mottars:**
- "Car Import Duty Calculator 2026" (interactive tool on mottars.com)
- "Cotonou vs. Lagos Port: Which Is Actually Cheaper in 2026?"
- "Complete Guide to Clearing Your Car at Nigerian Customs"
- Monthly/quarterly "Customs Rate Update" blog posts

### Topic E: Car Dealer Scams Nigeria

**Search Volume Indicators:** Extremely high engagement — Nairaland scam threads get thousands of views, multiple automotive blogs cover this topic, DreamCar has a dedicated 2025 buyers guide.

**Common Scam Types Documented:**

| Scam Type | How It Works |
|---|---|
| Fake online listings | Attractive photos on Jiji/Facebook, "booking fee" requested, scammer disappears |
| Odometer rollback | Mileage reduced digitally/manually to inflate value |
| Accidented car concealment | Heavy repainting, body filler, to hide crash damage |
| Forged customs papers | Fake documentation to sell stolen or smuggled vehicles |
| "First body" lie | Repainted car sold as never-repaired |
| Flooded car sale | Cars damaged by flooding sold after cosmetic cleanup |
| Advance payment fraud | Dealer requests deposit then becomes unreachable |
| Tokunbo/Nigerian used swap | Naija used car marketed as tokunbo at tokunbo prices |
| Fake urgency | "Another buyer is coming tomorrow" to pressure quick payment |
| Ghost dealership | Rented showroom that disappears after transactions |

**Common Dealer Lies (Exact Phrases):**
1. "Nothing to fix" — engine warning light appears later
2. "Just buy and go" — gearbox problems emerge immediately
3. "Foreign used but small accident" — extensive structural damage hidden
4. "Gearbox just needs service" — requires full rebuild
5. "Owner barely used it" — tired suspension, worn tyres tell different story
6. "AC is chilling" — gas leaks, compressor failure hidden
7. "It's first body" — evidence of repainting and panel misalignment
8. "The car just came in from abroad" — actually been sitting for months/years
9. "We only sprayed the bumper at the port" — entire car was repainted

## 6. COMPETITIVE CONTENT GAP ANALYSIS

### What Competitors Are Publishing
- **Jiji/Cars45:** Mostly SEO-driven listings pages, minimal educational content
- **Autochek:** Pan-African focus, premium-leaning guides, less Nigerian-specific
- **Carlots.ng:** Strong blog with Nigerian-specific content (comparison guides, buyer tips)
- **234Drive:** Good automotive journalism, inspection guides, model reviews
- **DreamCar.ng:** Price lists, scam guides, dealer directories

### Where Mottars Can Win
1. **Interactive tools** — No competitor has a proper import duty calculator, salary-based affordability calculator, or inspection checklist tool. Mottars can own this.
2. **Verified dealer narrative** — No competitor leads with trust/verification as aggressively. Every piece of content should reinforce this positioning.
3. **Split payment education** — CDCare and others have installment plans but none explain them well. Mottars can own the "pay small small" content category.
4. **Nigerian English / local voice** — Most competitor content reads generic. Content that uses tokunbo, Berger, "no wahala," pidgin casually will resonate more and feel authentic.
5. **Request-a-car content** — No competitor has content around "tell us what you want and we'll find it." This is a unique story to tell.
6. **Video/social-first** — Most competitors are blog-only. Short-form video (TikTok, Instagram Reels) about car scams, market visits, and inspection tips is an open field.

## 7. KEY TAKEAWAYS

1. **Trust is the #1 concern** — Every piece of content should address the fear of being scammed, either directly or by reinforcing Mottars' verification process.

2. **"Tokunbo" is the entry keyword** — It's the most distinctly Nigerian automotive search term and appears in nearly every buyer query. Mottars should own tokunbo content.

3. **Affordability drives all decisions** — Budget, maintenance cost, and financing options are more important than brand prestige for the target market. Content should always include price context.

4. **Interactive tools are the biggest gap** — Import duty calculators, affordability calculators, and inspection checklists are in high demand but poorly served by competitors.

5. **Nairaland is the primary community** — Not Reddit, not Quora. Content that answers Nairaland-style questions and uses Nairaland-style language will perform best.

6. **Split payment is an underserved content category** — High search interest but low-quality content available. Mottars' split payment feature maps directly to this demand.

7. **Scam content has the highest viral potential** — Stories about dealer lies, fraud experiences, and red flags consistently get the highest engagement across all platforms.

8. **Model-specific content scales well** — Price lists, maintenance costs, and common problems for specific car models are high-intent searches that can be programmatically generated.

9. **Location matters** — Lagos (Berger), Abuja, and the Cotonou import route are the three geographic anchors for content. City-specific guides will capture local search intent.

10. **The language of trust** — Use "verified," "genuine," "no wahala," "stress-free." Avoid "cheap" (implies low quality) and overly formal Western automotive terminology.
